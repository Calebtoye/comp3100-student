To the Honourable the Board of the Waterworks
14 March 1848

Gentlemen,

I beg leave to lay before the Board a small matter of method in the
Engine's working of Table IX, which I fear will not stay small.

The Engine rounds each culvert loss and each district balance to the
place the Table prints, and only then adds them. The computers have
always added first and rounded once, at the foot of the column. The
two methods agree only by luck. Where a figure ends in a five, the
Engine's way rounds it up, and every such figure adds its half to the
total. In the December quarter last I count two culvert figures and
four district figures so placed: the Engine's loss stands one
thousandth too high and its balance two pence too high, both in the
same direction, as a method errs and a slip of the pen does not.

The sums are not large. The habit is. A table that is wrong by a rule
is wrong in every quarter it is used, and will be trusted the more for
being consistent.

I would ask that Table IX be summed before it is rounded, as the
Guild's rule has always required, and that a computer check the foot
of each quarter by hand until it is.

I have the honour to remain, Gentlemen,
your diligent servant,

I. M. Kureos, Chief Computer
E.K.
